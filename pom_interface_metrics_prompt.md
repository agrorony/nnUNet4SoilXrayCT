## Literature basis for every metric below (read before implementing — do not invent alternative definitions)

- **Vogel, H.-J., Roth, K. (2001).** Quantitative morphology and network representation of soil pore structure. *Advances in Water Resources* 24(3–4), 233–242. — establishes Minkowski functionals (volume, **surface area**, integral mean curvature, Euler characteristic) as the standard morphological descriptor set for soil pore/solid structure from tomography; surface area is the 2nd Minkowski functional and the basis for specific surface area (SSA) as used below. This project's Euler characteristic pipeline already implements one functional from this same family (Vogel 1997 is already in the reading list for that) — SSA is the natural sibling functional, not a new methodology.
- **Houston, A.N., Otten, W., Baveye, P.C., Hapca, S. (2013).** Adaptive-window indicator kriging: A thresholding method for computed tomography images of porous media. *Computers & Geosciences* 54, 239–248. — defines solid–pore interfacial area via 3D Minkowski functionals on segmented CT images; this is the specific method cited below (Juyal et al. 2021) for a POM-adjacent study.
- **Juyal, A., Guber, A., Oerther, M., Quigley, M., Kravchenko, A. (2021).** Pore architecture and particulate organic matter in soils under monoculture switchgrass and restored prairie in contrasting topography. *Scientific Reports* 11, 21998. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-021-01533-7 — direct precedent: computes solid–pore interfacial area (via the Houston et al. 2013 Minkowski-functional method) in a POM-focused soil-CT study. This is the closest published analogue to what this prompt implements, and the recommended primary citation for the interface-area method in the draft.
- **Schlüter, S., Sheppard, A., Brown, K., Wildenschild, D. (2014).** Image processing of multiphase images obtained via X-ray microtomography: A review. *Water Resources Research* 50, 3615–3639. — reviews the two standard families of interfacial-area estimation from voxel data: (a) direct voxel-face counting (simple, but has a well-documented "staircase" bias that **underestimates** true surface area relative to a continuous surface), and (b) marching-cubes/Minkowski-functional mesh reconstruction (more accurate, matches the Vogel & Roth / Houston et al. convention above). This is why the plan below computes both and cross-checks them rather than picking one blind.
- **Herring, A.L., Andersson, L., Schlüter, S., Sheppard, A., Wildenschild, D. (2015).** *Advances in Water Resources* 79, 91–102. — already the source of this project's connectivity-density normalization convention (interfacial/topological quantity ÷ bulk sample volume, sign/scale chosen so larger = more of the property). Apply the identical normalization logic to get interfacial area **density** (µm² of interface per µm³ of sample) rather than inventing a new normalization.

Do not substitute other definitions of "interface" (e.g., Schlüter et al. 2022's distance-gradient or connected-pore-distance metrics) for the metrics below — those are already implemented elsewhere in this project (`pom_conditioning_and_psd_prompt.md`, Part A2) and answer a different question (proximity, not contact area). This prompt is specifically about measured contact/surface area between phases.

## Part 1 — Reproduce the existing voxel-face contact fraction (sanity anchor)

For each of the 3 volumes, using the same denoised POM mask (from `pom_analysis_20260815_light/<soil>/`) and the same pore/matrix labels (pore=5, POM=2; everything else = matrix, per `reading_masks_and_metrics.md`):

1. For every POM surface voxel (a POM voxel with ≥1 face-neighbor outside the POM mask, 6-connectivity for face-adjacency — same convention as the existing `pom_pore_contact_fraction`), classify each of its non-POM face-neighbors as pore (label 5) or matrix (everything else, including the minor "label 1" class — see caveat below).
2. Compute **POM–pore contact fraction** = (POM surface voxels with ≥1 pore-labeled face-neighbor) / (total POM surface voxels) — must reproduce the existing Bnei Re'em value (0.6423) within floating-point tolerance. If it doesn't match, stop and report the discrepancy before proceeding — don't silently use a different number.
3. Compute **POM–matrix contact fraction** the same way, substituting "matrix-labeled" for "pore-labeled." Report both fractions plus the overlap (POM surface voxels adjacent to both pore and matrix, since a surface voxel can face multiple neighbor types) so the two fractions' relationship to 100% is transparent rather than assumed to sum to 1.

## Part 2 — Marching-cubes surface area and its interface split

1. Reconstruct the denoised POM mask's surface via marching cubes — reuse the exact mesh-reconstruction code already used for sphericity in `pom_analysis_20260826_final_shape/scripts/run_pom_shape_clustering_final.py` (same algorithm, same library/parameters — no new sphericity-style script-version inconsistency like the one already logged in `PROJECT_STATUS.md`'s Track A history).
2. Compute **total POM surface area** (µm²) as the sum of triangle areas, at the object level and summed per volume.
3. **Split the surface by adjacent phase**: for each mesh triangle (or a dense sample of them), identify the nearest non-POM voxel just outside the surface and classify it as pore or matrix (same label convention as Part 1). Sum triangle areas by class to get **POM–pore interfacial area** and **POM–matrix interfacial area** (µm² each), per volume.
4. **Cross-check against Part 1**: the marching-cubes-based POM–pore area fraction (POM–pore area ÷ total POM surface area) should be in the same direction and roughly comparable magnitude to the voxel-face POM–pore contact fraction from Part 1 — expect the marching-cubes total surface area to be **larger** than a naive voxel-face-count surface area estimate (staircase bias, per Schlüter et al. 2014), but the *fraction* split between pore/matrix should be broadly consistent between the two methods. Report both numbers side by side and flag (don't silently resolve) any case where they disagree by more than 15 percentage points on the fraction.

## Part 3 — Specific surface area (SSA) and interfacial area density (IAD)

Compute both of the following, clearly distinguished — they answer different questions and must not be conflated in the report or the draft caption:

1. **Specific surface area (SSA)** = POM surface area ÷ POM volume (µm²/µm³, i.e. µm⁻¹; also report as mm²/mm³ for readability). This is a **POM-intrinsic shape/exposure descriptor** (how much surface a given amount of POM presents) — per Vogel & Roth's Minkowski-functional framework. Report: SSA_total (all POM surface), SSA_pore-facing (POM–pore area ÷ POM volume), SSA_matrix-facing (POM–matrix area ÷ POM volume), per volume.
2. **Interfacial area density (IAD)** = POM–pore interfacial area ÷ total sample (bulk) volume, and POM–matrix interfacial area ÷ total sample volume (µm⁻¹) — same per-bulk-volume normalization convention already used for connectivity density (Herring et al. 2015, already implemented in this pipeline's topology metrics). This is an **ecologically scaled quantity**: how much organo-pore or organo-mineral interface exists per unit volume of soil, which is what actually matters for comparing microbial-accessible interface area across soils with different POM abundance — as opposed to SSA, which normalizes away POM abundance entirely.
3. Do not fabricate a per-gram (m²/g) conversion unless a POM particle density is available and explicitly stated as an assumption with its source — if Rony wants that conversion later it can be added, but don't silently assume a density value.

## Part 4 — Object-level check (ties to existing findings)

Per volume, report what fraction of total POM–pore interfacial area (not volume — area) is contributed by the single largest POM object, and by the top 5 objects. This is the same kind of "is it dominated by a few large fragments" question already answered for POM *volume* (largest-object volume share, 17.1%/45.1%, already in the draft) — report the analogous number for *interfacial area*, since a large, blocky object and a smaller, highly convoluted one can contribute very differently to interface despite similar volume.
